## The rule, in plain words
- A short trim strip may be cut from the free space left on an earlier plywood sheet instead of starting a new sheet. A trim strip is a kick strip, toe kick or plinth, with its long side at most 48" and its short side at most a quarter of its length.
- Yard lays the sheets out both ways, with and without this, and keeps the one-sheet-cheaper layout. If both ways need the same number of sheets, nothing moves and the grain is kept.
- The strip goes into the earlier sheet as cut when it fits. It is turned across the grain only when that is the only way it fits. Its cut-list row then says "Cut across the grain from a sheet offcut", or "Cut 1 of 2 across the grain …" when only some of that row's pieces are turned.
- No other part (drawer backs, aprons, shelves, sides, doors) is ever moved or turned by this rule.
- Each saved sheet is −$38.43 on 3/4" plywood or −$42.00 on 1/2". Buy always matches the layout.
- I51 (report-only): a sheet holding nothing but trim strips that all fit earlier gaps is a violation. It does not block a build.
